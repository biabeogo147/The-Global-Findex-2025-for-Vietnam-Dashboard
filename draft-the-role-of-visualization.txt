The Vietnam Financial Inclusion Dashboard 2025 is engineered as an institutional, single-page interactive policy workspace. Its structural design converts 1,000 high-dimensional, weight-adjusted individual records from Findex_Microdata_2025_updateViet Nam.csv into five interconnected thematic layers. This design enables users to isolate structural inequities, track transaction flows, and diagnose systemic barriers across the Vietnamese financial ecosystem. 

Demographic baseline The dashboard begins with the Macro-Socioeconomic Baseline (Page 1). This section presents core KPI cards for the country, including urban concentration, secondary education thresholds, and workforce integration rates, alongside cross-tabulated population matrices. This step is placed first because it provides the user with an essential baseline for interpretation. All subsequent financial access indicators and transaction rates are viewed in relation to these basic population properties, meaning users must digest the country's demographic balance before analyzing specific financial variables.

Connectivity & infrastructure After establishing the demographic baseline, the dashboard shifts to Connectivity (Page 2). This section tracks smartphone ownership matrices, internet usage frequency, and foundational versus online digital identity verification rates. This phase is critical because modern digital financial inclusion is impossible without technology infrastructure. By examining connectivity depth early, the user can determine whether a lack of banking access is driven by an underlying lack of hardware and digital identification options.

Account ownership & landscape Once technology readiness is verified, the narrative moves to The Account Ownership & Access Landscape (Page 3). This section details official banking and mobile money inclusion metrics across income groups and uses a Sankey diagram to trace how rural and urban populations distribute across banking tiers. It also benchmarks the explicit institutional barriers faced by the unbanked. This step transitions the user from technical infrastructure to formal institutional access, highlighting exactly where the financial ecosystem falls short.

Cash flows & digitalization vectors The dashboard moves to Cash Flows & Digitalization Vectors (Page 4) to test whether account ownership actually changes daily transactional behavior. It maps cash versus digital delivery methods across wage inflows, agricultural sales, and public transfers, while tracking utility payments and consumer motivations for avoiding point-of-sale digital tools. This step helps users move from simple inclusion metrics to tracking active financial participation, showing where account holders continue to immediately liquidate their digital inflows into cash.

Financial health & resilience The final section focuses on Financial Health & Resilience (Page 5). It connects all previous layers - demographics, technology, accounts, and transaction choices - to evaluate how long households can cover expenses if they lose their primary income source, and maps what safety nets they use in an emergency. This deep dive is placed at the very end because household financial resilience can only be fully understood after analyzing the structural, technical, and behavioral factors that shape it.
The system is a hybrid explanatory-exploratory workspace. The vertical sequence of the pages drives an authored, policy-oriented argument: true financial inclusion cannot be evaluated by high-level account access alone; it requires evaluating infrastructure readiness, daily payment habits, and long-term economic resilience. Simultaneously, integrated interactive components allow users to break down the data and explore specific demographic subsets.
