\section{Chapter 2: Data Types, Marks, and Channels}
\label{sec:chapter2-marks-channels}

\subsection{Techniques / Principles Applied}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Classifying variables as nominal, ordinal, or quantitative before
encoding them.
\item Using color, width, and enclosure as supporting channels rather than the
primary source of exact comparison.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{How Applied in the Dashboard}

\subsubsection{Classifying Variables Before Encoding}

The dashboard first separates variables by role before assigning any visual
form.

\dashboardfigure{0.72}{fig-page1-socioeconomic-matrix.png}
{The socioeconomic matrix (income vs. education).}
{fig:socioeconomic-matrix}

\figref{fig:socioeconomic-matrix}{Socioeconomic matrix} treats income quintile
and education as ordered categories and uses grid cells positioned by row and
column to compare their intersections. Weighted population density is then
encoded through a sequential blue scale, which is appropriate because the page
needs to reveal concentration patterns rather than only isolated totals.

\dashboardfigure{0.82}{fig-page2-mobile-ownership.png}
{Mobile ownership rate by location, phone type, gender, and income group.}
{fig:mobile-ownership-profile}

\figref{fig:mobile-ownership-profile}{Mobile ownership profile} combines
subgroup categories such as location, gender, and income group with phone type
and access rate. Grouped bars and aligned lengths support accurate comparison
across small multiples, while hue separates basic phones from smartphones
without changing the baseline used for quantitative reading.

\dashboardfigure{0.72}{fig-page3-account-flow.png}
{The account composition flow.}
{fig:account-composition-flow}

\figref{fig:account-composition-flow}{Account composition flow} treats location
and account ecosystem type as nominal structural states connected by flows.
Nodes, horizontal position, ribbon width, and grouped color are used to show
how the population distributes across account conditions, which is more
important on this page than exact pairwise magnitude reading.

\dashboardfigure{0.88}{fig-page4-inflows.png}
{The national digitization of financial inflows: cash vs. digital delivery.}
{fig:national-inflows}

\figref{fig:national-inflows}{National inflow digitization} treats inflow type
as nominal categories and payment method as composition within each category.
The segmented 100\% stacked bars use aligned position and segment length to
show how wages, sales, pensions, and transfers split across cash,
direct-to-account, and other methods.

\subsubsection{Using Supporting Channels Carefully}

Supporting channels are kept secondary to the main quantitative comparison.
\figref{fig:socioeconomic-matrix}{Socioeconomic matrix} uses sequential blue
intensity to signal weighted density,
\figref{fig:account-composition-flow}{Account composition flow} uses ribbon
width and grouped color to organize structural flows, and
\figref{fig:save-borrow-trajectories}{Save vs. borrow trajectories} uses hue
to distinguish saving from borrowing once the reader has already located the
values by position.

\subsection{Notes / Adjustments}

On Page 5, \figref{fig:save-borrow-trajectories}{Save vs. borrow trajectories}
plots rates with a line-chart layout across ordered income quintiles and income
runway categories. Strict visualization theory usually reserves continuous
lines for truly continuous or time-series data, so connecting ordinal groups
can imply intermediate values that do not actually exist.

This dashboard keeps the line marks deliberately because they exploit spatial
continuity to make the behavioral trajectory easier to read. The lines show
that saving rises while borrowing falls as economic position improves, and that
contrast would be less legible in a denser bar-based layout. The adjustment is
therefore a controlled trade-off: it relaxes strict categorical encoding in
order to reveal the structural crossover between two financial strategies more
clearly.
