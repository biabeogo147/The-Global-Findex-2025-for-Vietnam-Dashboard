\subsection{Why This Dataset Was Chosen}

The Global Findex 2025 shows how mobile technology is assisting more adults worldwide to own and use financial accounts to participate in the internet and financial system. This is the world's first most comprehensive database on digital and financial inclusion and the only global source of comparable demand-side data (data collected directly from adults through surveys). The Global Findex 2025 serves as a vital resource for policymakers working on digital connectivity and financial inclusion, as well as for researchers, practitioners, and development experts.
The database was investigated by Development Research Group, Finance and Private Sector Development Unit in affiliation with World Bank and was produced by Gallup, Inc. 
The dashboard is based on the Global Findex 2025 microdata for Vietnam. Data was collected through face-to-face household interviews conducted between June 23 and August 31, 2024. A nationally representative sample of 1,000 individuals aged 15 years and older was obtained using a multistage probability sampling design. Survey weights were applied to correct for unequal selection probabilities and nonresponse bias, ensuring that estimates are representative of Vietnam's adult population. The survey has an approximate design effect of 1.33 and a margin of error of ±3.6 percentage points.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Q\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The Socioeconomic Exclusion Bottleneck:} Which specific intersecting demographic cohorts (cross-referenced by income quintile, gender, education, and rurality) remain entirely excluded from the formal financial framework, and what specific institutional friction logs (e.g., cost, distance, trust, or missing documentation) drive these unbanked constraints?

\item \textbf{The Digital Infrastructure Leapfrogging Paradox:} Is mobile money successfully expanding financial inclusion by reaching previously unbanked populations, or is it primarily serving as a secondary transaction tool for urban citizens who already have access to traditional banking?

\item \textbf{The Financial Inflow Digitization vs. Liquidation Vector:} How effectively do formal wage channels and public transfer frameworks digitize incoming financial flows, and what structural factors cause users to immediately liquidate these digital inflows into cash?

\item \textbf{Consumer Protection and Digital Literacy Gaps:} How does rapid smartphone and internet adoption across vulnerable, low-income or rural demographics correlate with exposure to digital scams, and does a lack of digital literacy create new security risks?

\item \textbf{Intersectional Financial Resilience Strategies:} How do financial access levels affect a household's ability to handle sudden economic shocks, and do marginalized groups rely more on formal savings or informal, high-risk community debt networks when facing a crisis?
\end{enumerate}
